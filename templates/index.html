The Index

What is the Human Development Index?

The HDI is a summary measure published by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) that ranks countries by more than just income. It condenses three dimensions of human wellbeing into a single score between 0 and 1, making it possible to compare long-term progress across very different economies.

Health Life expectancy at birth
Education Mean and expected years of schooling
Income Gross National Income (GNI) per capita

The Stakes

Why HDI matters

GDP alone can hide how ordinary people actually live — a resource-rich country can be wealthy on paper while its citizens have short lifespans or little access to education. HDI corrects for this by rewarding countries that convert income into longer, healthier, more educated lives.

Governments, researchers, and international organizations use HDI to track development progress, compare policy outcomes, and target investment where it is needed most.

Built on Four Signals

What the model looks at

Each prediction is built from the same four indicators the UNDP itself relies on, feeding a Linear Regression model trained on historical, cross-country data.

01

Life Expectancy

Average number of years a newborn is expected to live, given current mortality patterns. A proxy for healthcare quality and living conditions.

02

Mean Years of Schooling

The average number of years of education completed by adults aged 25 and older — a backward-looking measure of educational attainment.

03

Expected Years of Schooling

The years of schooling a child entering school today can expect to receive — a forward-looking measure of access to education.

04

GNI per Capita

Gross National Income per person, adjusted for purchasing power — a measure of the average material standard of living.

Σ

Linear Regression

The four indicators are standardized and combined through a trained Linear Regression model to produce a single continuous HDI estimate.

▤

Four Categories

The predicted score is mapped to a UNDP development tier — Low, Medium, High, or Very High — using the standard official thresholds.

Under the Hood

How a prediction is made

The same pipeline used to train the model runs — in miniature — every time you submit the form below.

1

Collect indicators

The dataset combines life expectancy, schooling, and income figures for a cross-section of countries, sourced from open HDI datasets.

2

Clean and prepare

Missing values are filled using column means, and the four features are selected as inputs with HDI as the prediction target.

3

Scale features

A StandardScaler normalizes each indicator so that no single feature — like GNI, which spans a much wider range than schooling — dominates the model.

4

Train Linear Regression

A Linear Regression model learns the relationship between the scaled indicators and the historical HDI score, evaluated with RMSE and R².

5

Predict and classify

Your submitted values are scaled with the same fitted scaler, passed through the trained model, and the resulting score is mapped to a development category.
